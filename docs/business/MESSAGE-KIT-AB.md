## 0. What this kit is for, and its one hard constraint

Two openings for the same conversation, rotated across the **twenty problem interviews already
planned**. The point is to find out whether the umbrella sentence attracts the buyer we want or a buyer
we will never serve, before we spend anything on saying it.

> **No new meetings.** Not one. This kit rides existing conversations. If it starts generating calendar
> entries of its own, risk R-8 has fired — positioning work displacing the actual mandate, which is
> three paid design partners by 30 November — and the kit is withdrawn, not defended.

**The constraint that outranks everything else in this file:** variant B contains the sentence, and the
sentence is registered as a **HYPOTHESIS with no cleared external use** pending the T-142 trademark
ruling. **Do not run variant B until that ruling is recorded.** Until then, run variant A in every
conversation and log `variant: A`. The kit is committed now so it is ready the day the ruling lands,
not so it can be used before.

## 1. Variant A — the wedge (current, cleared)

**Opening, 25 seconds:**

> "Your agents can already read anything. The question is what happens the first time one of them tries
> to *do* something — move money, change a limit, send a recommendation to a client. We decide that
> before it executes, against your policy, with a human in the loop where you want one. And afterwards
> there's a record your auditor can verify without asking us or you."

**The qualifying question:** *"Which agent, and which single action, is currently blocked on somebody
being uncomfortable?"*

**If they engage:** move to the use case in the catalogue, and offer the verifier. F-T-1 is counted
here — offer it every time, and log honestly whether they ran it.

## 2. Variant B — the umbrella (gated, do not use before the T-142 ruling)

**Opening, 25 seconds:**

> "Enterprise-grade, private, auditable AI for firms that can't afford to get it wrong. Concretely:
> your model, on your hardware, driving an agent whose every consequential action we decide on before
> it happens — and evidence afterwards that an auditor can check offline."

**The disclaimer is part of the script, not a recovery from it.** Say it in the same breath, unprompted:

> "To be clear on one thing up front, because it decides whether this is even relevant to you: **we
> don't supply the model. We govern yours.** No hosting, no fine-tuning, no GPU line on our invoice."

**The qualifying question is the same as variant A's.** Only the opening differs — otherwise we are
testing two conversations, not two sentences.

### Why the disclaimer sits *inside* variant B

Risk R-2: the word "AI" creates model-hosting expectations we will never meet. Putting the disclaimer
first makes the test **harder** on the positioning, not easier — anyone who still raises hosting after
being told plainly has an expectation the sentence created and the disclaimer could not undo. That is
exactly what F-T-7's first observable is trying to count, and it is why the log field counts only the
**unprompted first mention** (`PILOT-LOG.md` §2).

## 3. Rotation

* **Alternate by conversation in the order they occur** — first substantive conversation A, second B,
  third A, and so on. Do not sort by persona, seniority or promise; that is how a test gets a result
  the tester wanted.
* **Log the variant before the meeting, not after.** A variant recorded afterwards is a variant chosen
  afterwards.
* If a conversation goes to a second meeting, **the variant is fixed at the first**. Later meetings do
  not get a new row.
* Interviews run before the T-142 ruling are all variant A. They still count toward F-T-1, F-T-4, F-T-5
  and F-T-6; they simply cannot count toward F-T-7's variant-B observables. **Say so in the T-149
  decision record** rather than quietly reading a small B sample as a result.

## 4. What must be said the same way in both variants

Otherwise the comparison measures our delivery rather than the sentences.

| Ask | Answer — identical in A and B |
|---|---|
| "Do you host the model?" | No. We govern yours. Your model, your hardware or your account. No hosting, no tuning, no inference telemetry. There will not be a GPU line on our price list |
| "Is it enterprise-grade / production-ready?" | Not yet, and here is what is missing: install-by-a-stranger, restore drill, production end-to-end gate, and OIDC login. Those are in flight. It is a Technical Preview |
| "Is it private?" | It runs entirely inside your perimeter and reports nothing to us. Its only outbound connections are to endpoints you configure — your Vault, your timestamp authority. Data-level privacy of the exported evidence is a separate question and we have not finished it |
| "Are you compliant / approved / certified?" | No regulator has reviewed anything. We map controls; we do not certify. Cite the rule, show the control |
| "Can I check the evidence myself?" | Yes — here is the bundle and the command; two independent verifiers, neither of which contacts us. **Offer this every time**, and log whether they ran it |

The "enterprise-grade" answer is the one people will be tempted to soften **while variant B's opening
says the word out loud.** That tension is real and it is the honest cost of testing the sentence early;
the way through it is to say the word in the opening and correct it in the answer, not to soften the
correction.

## 5. Forbidden in both variants

Everything in the claims register §3, plus two specific to this test:

* **Do not defend the sentence.** If a prospect finds it vague or over-broad, that is the datum. Write
  it in `notes` and move to the qualifying question. Persuading them costs us the reading.
* **Do not show the T-144 recording before it exists.** It does not yet. When it does, T-148's partner
  probes are where it goes first, and the register row for "AI" must name the model and the scenario.

Counts live in `PILOT-LOG.md` §4 and are updated as rows are added, not reconstructed at the end. The
